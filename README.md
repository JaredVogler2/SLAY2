**Status as of 2026-11-02** (the source of truth is [docs/STATUS.md](docs/STATUS.md); nothing below is a claim beyond it):

* **Working and tested:** exact probability calculators for card-reward rarity with the hidden pity counter, potion drops, "?" rooms, draws, shop prices, card removal and gold (`/odds` and `POST /api/v1/odds`); **Route Lab** (`/routes`), which compares up to three typed routes through an act exactly (what the "?" rooms turn into, rare-card and potion odds, gold, relics) and never claims one is safer; and a manual card advisor that is a **labelled heuristic**, never a win probability (`/advisor`; it rates a card against the roles the deck lacks **and by how it works with the cards you already hold**: 385 cards are tagged by what they supply and what they need over 27 mechanics, the page names the engines your deck is building and the cards still waiting for support, and the starting relic counts as a supplier, as does any relic you list that provides a mechanic (that reading of relics is a judgement with no registered test; it was read once, as an exploration, against simulated runs that keep their relics). The strengths of that synergy term are a judgement, checked so far by an exploratory reading of 19,717 simulated runs (`/methods#synergy`: the term improves the held-out prediction of getting past the Act-1 boss; an engine credit has no support in those data, and a development stage on separate simulated seeds chose to keep none: the page names the engines a deck is building but its index does not count them); the one registered held-out test found that it improves what a simulated pilot picks (33.0 % of runs past the Act-1 boss against 26.0 % without the term, p = 3.8e-60, every class ahead; nothing about later bosses, winning or the live game), and the same test repeated in a simulator whose runs keep their relics gives 40.4 % against 33.3 % (p = 3.0e-58, every class ahead). It covers the draft, not the playing of cards against enemies): you say which Act 1 you are in (Overgrowth or Underdocks, with different elites and bosses) and what the opening Ancient gave you, its effect on your deck is applied as you report it, and the Act 1 version tilts the ranking by at most 30 % as its own labelled line, a judgement and not a measurement. Target: STS2 **main v0.107.1**; the beta v0.111.0 is refused. The pages are tested on phones, tablets and laptops (320 to 1280 px, enlarged text, no layout shift, no JavaScript) and make no request but to their own origin.

* **The Act-2 study (ADR-060; the registration is live since the merge of pull request #22 and the result since #23, `f6dd036`):** does anything about the card picks change how often a simulated run gets past the *second* boss? Three tests were registered before any game of them (each on the 2,000 held-out seeds of every class, relics kept, Ascension 0, five classes pooled into one exact test read at 0.05 / 3), a second analysis that must agree with the first was added during the development stage and before any held-out game, a development screen on other seeds chose which to play, and the choice was pushed before the first held-out game (30,000 games from a clean worktree; all 584 wins re-derived; 15 sample audits of 40 games; each pooled file recomputed by an independent script). **Result, published as it came out: a pilot that takes only cards worth 0.2 or more in every act got past the second boss in 11.40 % of 9,643 paired games against 10.49 % (+0.90 points, interval +0.16 to +1.65 at the registered level, exact p = 0.0052: passed), and past the first boss in 61.1 % against 66.9 %, so it trades first-boss odds for second-boss odds (all five classes are ahead at the second boss, one of them, Regent, clearly on its own, and all five are behind at the first); being pickier in the second act alone is not shown to help (10.82 % against 10.48 %, +0.34 points, interval -0.22 to +0.90, p = 0.17), and which acts carry the first rule's effect is not known; the refitted card values did not qualify in the screen and were not played; the advisor does not change.** Nothing about winning (the win counts are descriptive), the live game or a player is claimed.
* **The acts study (ADR-061; registered 2026-11-01, played and read 2026-11-02, published as it came out; live since the merge of pull request #25, `57bbb34`):** which act's card picks carry the effect of the old lead (take only cards worth 0.2 or more), and does it reach *winning*? Four tests were registered before any game of them (the lead, and a pilot that is pickier in Act 1 alone, in Act 2 alone and in Act 3 alone, each against the baseline that follows the card values the site shows) and played on 3,000 seeds of each class that no earlier study had used: 75,000 held-out games, relics kept, Ascension 0, the five classes pooled into one exact test per question, every one of 1,465 wins re-derived, 25 of 25 sample audits passed, each pooled file recomputed from the raw games by code that shares nothing with the harness. **Three of the four passed.** The lead won **2.48 % of its paired games against 1.68 %** (+0.80 points, exact p = 5.9e-10), at a price at the first boss (61.0 % of the paired games got past it, against 65.9 %). A pilot pickier in **Act 1 only** won 2.02 % against 1.68 % (+0.35 points) at the same price. A pilot pickier in **Act 2 only** won **1.95 % against 1.66 %** (+0.29 points, interval +0.04 to +0.53, exact p = 0.0046) with **no price at the first boss** (identical by construction) and none shown at the second. **Act 3 only was not shown** (+0.06 points, p = 0.43), the outcome the registration expected, since few runs get that far. The gains are small and two of them pass narrowly (the lower ends of their intervals are 7 and 6 games in 14,500 pairs); nearly all the wins are Ironclad's and Defect's; the simulator has never been compared with the live game; and 0.2 is the only threshold tested. **The advisor does not change:** the owner left the question to the project, which decided on 2026-11-02 not to offer a pickier mode yet (the evidence is narrow and the threshold 0.2 was never compared with another); the next registered study repeats the Act-2 test on new seeds and screens thresholds, and the advisor is revisited when it has been read (ADR-061, addendum B). The record (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Result of ADR-061") says what I had seen before the numbers were read and owns a flaw of its own power paragraph (it guessed too few discordant pairs for the Act-3 test); the pages are `/insights#acts` and `/methods#acts`.
* **The threshold study (ADR-062; registered 2026-11-02 before its first game, in pull request #28; played and published as it came out the same day; the site shows it once this change is merged):** does the Act-2 result of ADR-061 hold on seeds nobody has played, and is another threshold better? A development screen of six thresholds of the card reward of Act 2 (0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.45, 0.6 and never taking a card) on 3,000 fresh seeds of each of Ironclad and Defect (42,000 games) advanced nothing: every threshold had more net wins than the baseline in development, 0.45 the most (+65), and it was ahead of 0.2 head to head by 18 net wins (103 against 85) but not at the registered p below 0.20 (p = 0.215). So the held-out run was one replication of 0.2 against the baseline on 15,000 other fresh seeds of each class (60,000 games, all 2,508 wins re-derived, pooled once). **It passed: 0.2 in Act 2 won 4.55 % of the paired games against 4.06 % (1,308 against 1,166 in 28,727 pairs both games completed), +0.49 points of the win share, whole-seed interval +0.28 to +0.71, 558 pairs won only by the arm against 416, exact p = 6.0e-06; the analysis of every pair agrees (p = 1.4e-06).** The point estimate is about two thirds of what ADR-061 saw on its own seeds (the two are not distinguishable), with no price at the first boss (by construction); pooled over the two classes, Ironclad carries it (+0.72 points) and Defect points the same way (+0.27, not shown alone at 0.01). It does not say why, that 0.2 is the best threshold (the held-out run tested no other), or anything about the live game: every number is a rules pilot in the pinned simulator. The record also discloses that both arms' raw win totals in Defect (534 and 579) were seen before pooling. **The advisor's own recommendation does not change.** ADR-061's addendum B and item 8 (iii) of the record allowed, after a passed replication, an opt-in, labelled simulated second opinion for 0.2 in Ironclad and Defect, and ADR-063 adds it as its own change (next bullet).

* **The advisor's price of a slot (ADR-065; index v2, 2026-11-02):** the owner wrote that the advisor "always recommends taking cards" and that skipping is sometimes best because a bigger deck draws the cards you need less often. They were right: the index only grew as cards were added, and the pilot that played card rewards by it skipped 0.4 % of them. It now charges one index point for every card from the 21st on (a judgement, chosen with the owner's point and the evidence that picking fewer cards wins more for pilots that follow measured values; not a measurement), so on 7,785 card-reward states from simulated runs skipping ranks first in 7.5 % of them instead of 0.7 % (Act 1 0.6 %, Act 2 22.5 %, Act 3 40.4 %), and the option's reasons say so. **A registered check asks only whether the price does harm where a simulation can see:** the pilot that plays by the index gets as far as with version 1's price in each Act 1 (20,000 games): a screen for gross harm, margins of a point at the first boss and a quarter of a floor. It cannot show a benefit (that pilot wins about one game in 600: 34 of the 20,000 games of the check). **It is played (2026-11-03, 20,000 held-out games, published as it came out): both legs are not behind, so the price does no gross harm as far as the simulation can see.** In Overgrowth the pilot that charges for a slot got past the first boss as often as with version 1's price (40.46 % against 40.41 %, +0.05 points, interval -0.18 to +0.29) and reached the same floors (+0.007, interval -0.04 to +0.05), skipping 6.5 % of its card rewards instead of 0.7 %. SLAY2 is an independent, unofficial, Vercel-hosted **coaching and decision-intelligence website** for players of Mega Crit's **Slay the Spire 2**. It is **not a browser clone or redistribution of the game**. The product combines conditional probability calculators, context-sensitive strategy guidance, validated fair-information simulators, search, reproducible machine learning and outstanding premium UI/UX. Gameplay is in the user's legitimately installed copy of STS2. Manual entry works without an account, mod, or subscription.

## Quickstart

**First-time player (no install):** open the site's **Odds** page, pick *Card rewards*, say how many non-rare cards you have seen since your last rare (or list the packs you have seen) and read the exact chance of a rare in your next pack; the fraction behind every number is one click away. On **Advisor** choose your character, enter your deck and the offered cards and read the ranking with its reasons and how stable it is. Your run lives in your browser only; export it as JSON whenever you like.

**Advanced player or competitor:** `/routes` (Route Lab) compares up to three routes through an act exactly, in the link, so a route can be shared; `POST /api/v1/odds` and `POST /api/v1/recommend` are the same engines as JSON (`GET /api/v1/capabilities` lists what is supported and what is not; every answer says which build and which assumptions it rests on); `/methods` shows every rule with its evidence, every simulated batch with its sample and interval, and the known gaps of the simulator. To check a published number yourself, follow [docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md](docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md) (seeds, hashes, the exact commands, what can and cannot be reproduced without the third-party simulator).

**Developer / competitor (local):**

```bash
node -v                                  # 22.x (see .node-version); pnpm 10.28.0 is pinned by package.json
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
pnpm --filter @slay2/web dev             # http://localhost:3000
pnpm check                               # typecheck, lint, prettier, all unit tests, production build
pnpm --filter @slay2/web e2e             # browsers: PW_CHROMIUM_PATH=/path/to/chromium if Playwright's own is not installed
curl -s localhost:3000/api/v1/capabilities
curl -s -X POST localhost:3000/api/v1/odds -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"calculator":"draw","pile":10,"targets":1,"draws":5}'   # {"n":"1","d":"2"}: exactly 1/2
```

The simulation harness (`sim/`, Python) needs a locally fetched, pinned third-party simulator that is **never committed**; see [docs/IP-COMPLIANCE.md](docs/IP-COMPLIANCE.md) and `scripts/external-fetch.sh`.

**Documentation:** [STATUS](docs/STATUS.md) (what exists, levels by cohort, next steps) · [RELEASE-AUDIT](docs/RELEASE-AUDIT.md) · [TEST-REPORT](docs/TEST-REPORT.md) · [MODEL-AND-DATA-CARDS](docs/MODEL-AND-DATA-CARDS.md) · [REPRODUCIBILITY](docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md) · [DEPLOY](docs/DEPLOY.md) · [IP-COMPLIANCE](docs/IP-COMPLIANCE.md) · [DECISIONS](docs/DECISIONS.md).

**Privacy and IP:** no accounts, cookies or analytics; the pages do not send your run anywhere and the public API stores nothing. SLAY2 is unofficial and non-commercial, not affiliated with or endorsed by Mega Crit; it ships no game art, card text or audio (the card catalog holds facts only: ids, names, class, rarity, cost, type and SLAY2's own role tags). The code has no licence file yet (an owner decision): it is visible, not licensed for reuse. ## Critical differences from SLAY (original game)

- STS2 released Early Access March 5, 2026; official game built using **Godot with C#**, with native mod support. The initial scope is the STS2 official default Steam channel **v0.107.1**; latest separately recorded beta **v0.111.0** as checked October 2026; always re-verify on startup. **Never combine main/beta data or training artifacts.**
- Five characters: **Ironclad, Silent, Regent, Necrobinder, Defect**. Class-specific mechanics include Regent Stars/Forge/Sovereign Blade, Necrobinder Osty/Souls/Doom/Graveyard/Ethereal, Silent Sly, Defect orb systems, and Ironclad unique new cards.
- STS2 currently uses **three acts**, with alternate Act 1 **Overgrowth / Underdocks**, new Ancients and relic-choice systems. Highest current Ascension **A10**, not A20, and A10 requires an Act 3 double-boss clear. **No STS1 Heart/keys/Act 4 assumptions.**
- Solo first, with **2–4 player co-op** a separately validated workstream: multiplayer-only cards, ally targeting, synchronized decisions, scaling, seat-level outcomes. Do not promise co-op parity until tested.
- First-ever tutorial/progression runs can have scripted rewards and Neow availability. Ancients/unlocks/Epoch Timeline and reward changes may alter observation and game state.
- Everything changes across Early Access builds: card values, prices, monsters, AI moves, rarities, event eligibility, RNG and even mod protocols. A model that works on the wrong patch should abstain or fall back.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **No fabricated facts**: never claim simulations, trained models, win rates, parity, website deployments, or probability certainty without checked artifacts and explicit version, cohort and sample.
2. **Honest information**: visible-only policy; raw mod/engine RNG state, future draw order, hidden map/rewards, scripted scenario seed and unseen intents MUST NOT leak to player-facing agents.
3. **Real game experimentation**: `1000 combat playouts` ≠ `1000 completed games`. Validate full-run outcomes against real STS2 before trusting simulated win rates.
4. **Research-specific rights**: Mega Crit retains game IP. Independent original UI art; no bundling extracted art/C# or bulk publishing copyrighted content. Verify data API terms/third-party repository licenses before copying.
5. **Hybrid intelligent coaching**: exact probability calculators when valid; robust rules & search; neural networks **only when measured better** on fair matched held-out runs.
6. **No serverless training jobs**: Vercel hosts the site and lightweight APIs, intensive training and full-run simulations run on local/isolated workers.
7. **Incremental resilience**: commits after small passing steps, status/evidence update after every stage; resume from real repository state.
